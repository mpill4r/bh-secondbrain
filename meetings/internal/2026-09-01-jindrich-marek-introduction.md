## Key Discussion Points

### Backgrounds

Marek previously worked at Sublabs (a Košice-based agency, unfamiliar to Jindřich), running end-to-end delivery himself — discovery, client, delivery, upsell, testing, hours tracking (retainer vs. time-and-material) — with no dedicated project manager. He mentioned he records all his meetings and processes them into his own personal knowledge system ("second brain") — i.e. this very note is a direct output of that habit.

Jindřich has been at the client (referred to as "Max" throughout, i.e. Dr. Max) about a week, split across two workstreams ("Digab" and "Max"). He was hired as an AI manager for both projects and AI adoption. Dr. Max's CIO, **Tomáš Dudaško**, needs clear arguments that initiatives are happening, happening well, and justify continued investment.

### Jindřich's three-silo mandate

1. **Straighten out the projects** — consolidate reporting and timelines so status doesn't require a weekly hour-long sync; feed Dudaško the reporting material he needs to report upward. Also assess whether current FTE capacity is enough to deliver, or whether more people/streams are needed — Jindřich expects the account to need a dedicated project/product person as it grows, especially once other vendors (e.g. Deloitte) get pulled in alongside BigHub.
2. **Drive AI adoption across the whole business** — not just deliver features, but build visible momentum: newsletters, workshops, campaigns, a "hype" that gets business users pitching their own use cases (Jindřich's example: someone in accounting manually processing invoices could be a use case).
3. A third silo Jindřich said he'd thought of but **forgot to state** — flagged as an open item.

### Current project landscape (Marek's initial read)

Backlog currently lives in a Word document. Known projects/threads so far: **MaxBuddy** (AI assistant, blocked on some regulatory/`nariadenia` issue), **Lexia/Maxi** (a vendor swap situation), **reklamace** (complaints — seems largely resolved), and **Listing**, which Marek understood — from Alana and the developers — to be the current top priority: an enrichment layer on top of existing product data, governed by rules, essentially an "upkeep" flow.

### Spec structure agreed (Spec 1 / Spec 2)

Jindřich's proposed structure for the shared "Jindra Space" (SharePoint): one column/entry per project holding **Spec 1** — the raw specification as-is, copied without alteration from source (per project, one spec) — and, once ready, **Spec 2** — a more developed version, potentially closer to Marek's own product vision for it. Marek confirmed the structure works for him. Before work moves into tickets, each spec should be argued and framed at the start: why the initiative is being run and what result it should produce (e.g. the chatbot saves N minutes per request) — a simple table, not a rigorous analysis, is enough for the business to follow the logic. At the end, a similarly lightweight measurement approach closes the loop. Business owns and must validate these numbers; BigHub only checks them, doesn't own them.

Once confirmed and framed, work moves into their workflow as tickets and gets broken down further.

Jindřich's own approach: not chasing deep technical understanding yet, just confirming people know what to do, the client is satisfied, and things are moving — deeper technical involvement comes later.

Honza Sovka is expected to document this — it's currently completely undescribed. Jindřich confirmed this is a real analysis-blocking priority, separate from a related but distinct client-side "vendorský portál" initiative (not owned by BigHub, possibly not built by anyone yet) meant to let suppliers submit listings directly.
