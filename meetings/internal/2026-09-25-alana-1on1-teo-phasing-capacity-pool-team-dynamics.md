## TL;DR

Alana reframed the TEO/OCR Excel question. Service protocols arrive only twice a year (spring and autumn), and the autumn 2026 cycle is already running, so moving off Excel now makes no sense. The agreed direction is phased: Excel covers the current cycle, and a non-Excel review solution gets built before the spring 2027 cycle. Marek will consolidate Jura's comments and first test Radim Švarc's appetite for further build in an informal call. Radim is the entry point; Tomáš Burda holds the decision. Alana also clarified that BigHub's Dr. Max capacity is a flexible annual pool of about 3 FTE (~60 MD/month) that has built up unused budget since May. The two also talked through the week's team frictions (Jindřich's working style, Jakub Turner) and Dudaško's positive reaction to Thursday's meeting.

## Key Discussion Points

### TEO/OCR: the twice-yearly cycle changes the Excel question

Marek asked whether he needed more context before acting on Jura Brázdil's comments. Jura's comments stress the benefits of *not* staying on Excel, and they respond to the 2026-09-24 one-pager for Alana (`documents/internal/2026-09-24-teo-ocr-excel-vystup-ai-framing-odporucania-radim.md`). Alana's earlier comment was that Dr. Max won't rush into anything new. Before pitching anything, Marek wants to gauge how resistant Radim is to building something further. He added that, for BigHub, a frontend is no longer an expensive or long piece of work for Jura.

Alana raised the practical constraint:
- The service protocols come in only **twice a year, in autumn and spring**. The samples BigHub received were from spring 2025, autumn 2025 and spring 2026. The autumn 2026 cycle is **already running**.
- So pushing the review step off Excel now is pointless for this cycle. BigHub has until the **spring 2027 cycle** to build something that isn't Excel.
- She also asked: "why would they even want Excel? It doesn't make sense." [translated from Slovak]

Marek said he had missed the twice-yearly cadence and that it is a real factor. He proposed a phased framing: Phase 1 covers the current cycle, and the longer-term "long shot" is the fuller solution. Alana agreed.

**Is it worth the investment?** Marek recalled Jan Sovka repeatedly warning that something done only once or twice a year may not justify investment, and asked whether anyone knows how long the manual work actually takes per cycle (for example, a month). Alana didn't know. Marek's view: in the end it's a client, and if hours are available to spend, it's fine. (For comparison, the BQ interview on 2026-09-15 estimated ~250 protocols/week and ~40 h / ~5 MD per month. That figure doesn't match a twice-yearly burst model; see Open Questions.)

### TEO/OCR: the discovery gap and the relationship opportunity

Alana explained how the use case started. Radim Švarc cold-called her, described a twice-yearly service with a large volume of manually processed documents, and suggested it might suit automation. It stopped there. For the other use cases, Jan Sovka went in and interviewed people and shaped the use cases himself. Nobody has done that discovery for TEO: BigHub never asked how the whole process works or what else happens in it.

- Alana wants to **work out the rest of the process with them**. It looks like a success, they'll likely want to keep working with BigHub on this stream, "but we don't know in which direction." [translated from Slovak] Examples of possible extensions: tracking when the next service is due.
- She stressed the value of a quick, visible win: fast delivery and good results bring positive feedback for free. Compare the voicebot and other streams, which "drag on for a year." [translated from Slovak]
- Marek read this as a door opening, whether on this stream or on a new one with the same team, and worth pursuing.

### TEO/OCR: stakeholder path, from Radim to Burda

Alana: Radim is young and capable, but he has no decision-making say on where this goes next. That sits with **Tomáš Burda**, who is older and "pricklier". Marek: Radim is the foot in the door, since he'd never get to Burda directly. If he wins Radim over, it opens that path. Alana agreed: "Radim is a good entry point." [translated from Slovak]

Marek's plan: consolidate Jura's comments, have a non-committal call with Radim to test how opposed he is to further build or innovation, then develop the proposal from there.

### Capacity: the ~3 FTE pool is shared and flexible

Marek asked how BigHub's Dr. Max capacity works. He described it as roughly 3 FTE split across part of Filip, part of Jura, Marek himself and maybe half of Jindřich. His question was whether the team can direct that capacity freely. The context: Filip Černý had recently asked about doing something extra, and Marek didn't know whether that would overspend or take capacity from something else.

Alana:
- Yes, broadly. The budget was sized at **3 FTE for a full year from about May**. Little work happened in the early months, so there should be **accumulated, unused money**. Going somewhat over in a given month is fine.
- That comes to roughly **3 × 20 = ~60 MD/month**, and **the direction it's spent on doesn't matter**. Her words: "unless Tomáš says he wants us to do nothing on TEO at all" [translated from Slovak], in which case the time goes elsewhere.
- The exact accumulated amount is unknown. **Jindřich could find out from Tomáš.**
- She was unsure whether **Jindřich is inside the 3 FTE or on top of it** (making it 4). She herself was counted in the 3 FTE while on the account. Jindřich joined full-time, so she suspects he's extra. He showed her a September capacity table, so he'd know.

Marek now understands the capacity as shared and sized properly, so ideas like Filip's are less of a risk. He'll confirm the details with Jindřich.

### Team dynamics: Jindřich's working style

Several team members had called Alana the previous day to talk through frictions. Marek had also had separate talks that morning with Filip and with Jindřich.

What Marek said he told Jindřich (framed as a talk, not a work meeting):
- **Estimates**: he can't message the devs in the morning, expect an estimate by afternoon, put it in a management deck, and leave Jura's name under a number Jura doesn't understand. Devs need more room.
- **Meeting notice**: BigHub's culture is flexible about hours. A meeting with an hour's notice can collide with other plans, and it could often just move to the next day.
- **Phone calls**: Marek asked Jindřich not to call his mobile for things that could be a message. When Marek is working he's on Teams.
- **Deadlines and pressure**: no need to send the devs deadlines and "go comment on this" requests. People here are responsible, and if left some latitude, the work gets done. Otherwise the risk is frustrated devs leaving.
- Jindřich said he understood. He attributed his style to his consulting background and acknowledged he has to learn this environment. Marek is cautiously optimistic.

Alana's view:
- Jindřich is a **very good fit for Dr. Max**, which is corporate-heavy and ticket-first, with decisions passing through 20 people. That's exactly why she didn't take on that style herself.
- BigHub works very differently. Devs on corporate projects suffer under 6 meetings a day and ~2 productive hours. Devs who've been around longer are used to Alana's and Kuba Vodička's style.
- Jindřich needs to find a balance. But the devs also have to adapt as BigHub takes on bigger clients: "neither of us can stay frozen in one style." [translated from Slovak]

Marek's example of how latitude works: Dudaško had asked for a design, and Jura refused to work from Marek's initial version. Marek gave him a brief instead, and Jura came back with exactly what the meeting needed, which Dudaško praised strongly.

### Team dynamics: Jakub Turner

## Decisions Made

- **TEO/OCR stays on Excel for the autumn 2026 cycle.** No push to change the review step mid-cycle. A non-Excel review solution becomes a Phase 2 target, to be ready before the **spring 2027** cycle. Phased framing agreed by Marek and Alana.
- **Radim first, then Burda.** Marek tests Radim Švarc's appetite informally before any proposal. Radim is treated as the entry point to Tomáš Burda, the actual decision-maker.
- **The ~3 FTE (~60 MD/month) pool is flexible across streams.** Marek can direct dev capacity (e.g., Filip's ideas) without per-item approval, within the pool, and occasional monthly overruns are acceptable given the budget accumulated since May.
