### Dr. Max — the client

- Czech pharmacy chain, official entity **ČLH (Česká lékárna holding)**, part of **Penta**. ~40 billion CZK revenue in Czech, ~10% (~4 billion CZK) from the e-shop.
- Grew via acquisitions; a group called **BDC** oversees local entities globally, but local entities (like the Czech one) retain significant autonomy. Some infrastructure is global, some local — creates friction/bottlenecks (e.g. approvals, access).
- Relationship began years ago via a hackathon-style engagement that produced the original **MaxBuddy** concept.
- Because of Penta ownership, the account is numbers-driven — decisions justified with business cases and ROI, not goodwill.

### MaxBuddy — origin and pivot

- Original idea: pharmacists, not doctors, are legally responsible for correct dispensing. MaxBuddy was built to assist pharmacists with dosage verification at the point of sale (e.g. pediatric antibiotic syrup dosing based on the patient's weight), pulling from SPC reference data.
- Took ~9 months to reach production across ~3-4 months of iteration plus infrastructure/approval delays. Rolled out to 20 test pharmacies — at which point legal flagged the dosage-check feature as a medical device requiring costly certification/auditing. Dr. Max decided it wasn't worth pursuing certification.
- The feature that survived and shipped: AI-driven cross-sell ("psí prodeje" — upsell suggestions) at the point of sale, based on basket contents, with sales-style copy generated by the system. Rolling out to all ~600 pharmacies within about a month; expected to move ~0.5% on ~40B CZK revenue — a significant business case.
- MaxBuddy's dosage-check logic still exists "dormant in the code" — could be revived if Dr. Max later decides to pursue certification. Currently their call, and BigHub is respecting it despite disagreeing.

### Current active project streams (9 total, one client)

1. **MaxBuddy cross-sell** — see above; nearing full rollout.
2. **Call center automation** — voicebot; currently blocked on a vendor not providing needed data/access; needs a workaround.
3. **Warehouse / claims (reklamace) automation** — most complex stream; requires integration with Dr. Max's legacy warehouse system **Axapta** (a repurposed, warehouse-specific fork of Microsoft Dynamics) via custom-built APIs. Spec'd to the greatest level of detail of any stream. Surfaced a major process-reality gap late in development: the spec was based on the Prague warehouse, but discovered ~2 months before go-live that 3 other warehouses (including Brno) follow a different, paper-based process — the process owner hadn't accounted for it.
4. **Delivery/freight invoicing automation** — drivers currently process paper invoices manually; each vendor has different (apparently informally-agreed) claims terms, which complicates automation.
5. **Listing automation** — generating/standardizing product listings for e-shop and in-store (currently ~6-8 people manually copying between ChatGPT and back for quality); a lightweight, faster alternative to a "real" centralized product catalog Dr. Max has wanted for years. Notable: Filip Černý is bringing informal product judgment to this stream even without a formal product title.
6. **E-commerce demand/predictive model** — replaces a "look at yesterday's data" campaign-planning approach with a near-real-time model that flags gap-to-target and recommends campaign sizing, aiming to avoid both under- and over-spending on promotions.
7-9. Three further streams referenced only at a high level (Jan will follow up with detail on request) — general theme: the 9 streams exist because work repeatedly got blocked on infrastructure/approvals on one stream, so the team picked up a new one rather than stall.

Jan noted that if all 9 streams ran at full pace simultaneously, the current team could not keep up — they'd need more people.

### Working method — coached by Jan

- **"Old wise man" discovery approach**: start with people who've been at the company a long time and understand the *why* behind existing processes, rather than immediately proposing "you're doing this wrong." Look for internal champions who can explain historical/regulatory/acquisition-driven reasons before assuming something is just broken.
- **Go both directions**: gather management's priorities/KPIs (to understand motivation and how to pitch value), and separately talk to the people actually doing the work — reality on the ground frequently differs from what management believes (concrete example: the warehouse claims process, where a Brno warehouse turned out to still run on paper notebooks, unknown to the process owner).
- **Prototype-driven specs**: Jan's approach has been to show business stakeholders clickable/FigJam-style prototypes before or alongside writing specs — this surfaced edge cases the team wouldn't have anticipated from a text brief alone, and got much better buy-in than a written two-paragraph brief. Marek confirmed this matches his own prior experience (going from a Scrum/text-brief background to Camunda-style/prototype-and-AC-driven specs) and sees strong value in it.
- Everything discussed on calls is recorded on Dr. Max's own Teams (client-managed infrastructure); Jan and team feed transcripts/recordings into an LLM for processing. Marek will need to be provisioned access to Dr. Max's Teams (their instance has aggressive 24h auto-logout with no warning) — until then, Jan will share offline copies of key materials directly.

### Selling internally / avoiding an "IT delivers, business blames IT" dynamic

Jan described actively working to dissolve the traditional "IT delivers something, business just consumes it and blames IT when it breaks" dynamic at Dr. Max. His approach: get business stakeholders to feel co-ownership (e.g. escalating infrastructure blockers through the business stakeholder who has skin in the outcome, not just through IT), and actively "sell" delivered value back to the business — workshops, demos, presenting on the board with before/after framing (mentioned: today's listing demo on the board, built from ~6 hours of investment — a video from Filip plus a deck from Jan). Passive delivery (e.g. one email, then silence, as happened with an early call-center UAT that got zero engagement in 14 days) doesn't work — needs active promotion, workshops, and reminders.

### Success criteria and long-term ambition

- **~1 month**: Jan wants to see that Marek (with Jindřich) has full visibility and is actively driving all 9 project streams — not that things are just drifting forward on their own.
- **Longer-term**: deepen and extend the engagement — e.g. a new inventory/procurement planning system (worth billions annually) — and, described as the biggest prize ("golden ticket"), export the *methodology* (not the Alfred tool itself) to other Dr. Max country markets as new teams (Italy, Romania, etc.), driven by proven success in Czech.
- Contrast case: a newer Austrian client is currently trying to direct BigHub's approach in ways that aren't working well — reinforces the value of BigHub holding its ground on process when justified.
